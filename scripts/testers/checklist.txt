WHAT TO TRY (about 20 minutes)
------------------------------
Do these in order, in a project of your own if you like. Write down anything
that surprises you, even small things: that's exactly what we want to hear.
FEEDBACK.txt in this folder has a space for each step.

1. Open it. Put Flowstate on a MIDI / instrument track and open its window.
   You see a dark window: a header, "What do you want to make today?" with
   some starters, and a prompt box at the bottom.

2. It follows your DAW. The Tempo and Meter chips near the top show your
   project's tempo and meter. Change the tempo (say to 97): it follows
   within a second.

3. Make an idea. Type what you want in the prompt box (for example "moody
   lo-fi chords with a lazy beat") and press Return. Or click Surprise me.
   A first sketch plays almost at once; the AI's parts replace it lane by
   lane within a few seconds. Press play in your DAW: the idea plays in time
   with your song, through Flowstate's built-in sounds.

4. Make it yours:
   - Each lane has Mute (M), Solo (S), Lock, Vary (sparkle), Re-roll (arrows)
     and a density knob. Vary asks the AI for a new version of that part;
     Re-roll plays the same part a different way, instantly; the knob makes
     it busier or sparser. A locked part never changes.
   - With an idea open, type a change in the prompt box, like "darker" or
     "half-time drums", and press Return.
   - Tweak (in the toolbar) has instant changes: simplify, intensify,
     revoice, octave and semitone up or down.
   - Add part (under the lanes) adds a bass, melody, chords and so on.
   - Undo and Redo work, and the thread (the menu button) lists every
     version; click one to go back to it.

5. Use what you played. With your DAW playing, play a short riff (2 or 4
   bars) on your keyboard into Flowstate's track. Then open "Use what I
   just played" and choose, say, Add drums or Add bass. Your riff stays
   exactly as you played it, with the new part around it.

6. Library. Open Library (top of the window), preview a clip while the DAW
   plays, and Use it.

7. Drag it into your song. Drag a lane's handle (the dots on its left) onto
   a track in your DAW, or the toolbar's handle for all parts at once. You
   get a normal MIDI clip. Put it on one of your own instruments.

8. Save and reopen. Save your project, close it, open it again. The idea,
   its versions and the window size come back.

9. The space bar. Click a button in Flowstate, then press Space: your DAW
   starts or stops as usual, and the button isn't pressed. Typing in the
   prompt box never triggers DAW shortcuts.

Not in this build yet: editing single notes in a lane (that button is dimmed;
hovering it says why). Anything else that's dimmed or says "isn't available",
please note it.

If something goes wrong, Settings (the gear, top right) shows the build
number; it's also at the top of FEEDBACK.txt.
